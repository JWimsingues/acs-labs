= Exercise 6: Splunk integration
:navtitle: Exercise 6: Splunk integration

Estimated time: 15 minutes.

RHACS has a native Splunk integration that forwards policy violation alerts to Splunk via the *HTTP Event Collector* (HEC).
Once configured, every alert triggers a Splunk event that your SOC or platform team can search, correlate, and build dashboards around.

== Task 6.1: Locate the Splunk HEC endpoint

The Showroom environment includes a Splunk instance. First, find the HEC URL and create a token.

. On the bastion, get the Splunk web console URL:
+
[source,bash]
----
oc get route splunk-web -n splunk -o jsonpath='https://{.spec.host}'
----
+
. Open the Splunk console in a browser and log in:
** *Username:* `admin`
** *Password:* `RhacsSplunkDemo123!`
. Navigate to *Settings* > *Data inputs* > *HTTP Event Collector*.
. If no HEC token exists, create one:
** Click *New Token*.
** *Name:* `acs-alerts`
** Click *Next* through the wizard (accept defaults).
** On the review page, click *Submit*.
** Copy the *Token Value* — you will need it in the next task.
. Note the HEC endpoint URL. From inside the cluster, it follows the pattern:
+
[source,text]
----
https://splunk-hec-splunk.apps.cluster-sz6zn.dyn.redhatworkshops.io/services/collector
----
+
You can also find the HEC route:
+
[source,bash]
----
oc get route splunk-hec -n splunk -o jsonpath='https://{.spec.host}/services/collector'
----

NOTE: If the `splunk-hec` route does not exist, the HEC endpoint may be exposed through the main Splunk service on port 8088. Check with `oc get svc -n splunk`.

== Task 6.2: Configure the Splunk notifier in ACS

. In the RHACS Console, navigate to *Platform Configuration* > *Integrations*.
. Scroll to *Notifier Integrations* and click *Splunk*.
. Click *Create integration*.
. Fill in the fields:
+
[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
|Field |Value

|Integration name
|`Lab - Splunk HEC`

|HTTP Event Collector URL
|_(paste the HEC URL from Task 6.1)_

|HTTP Event Collector token
|_(paste the HEC token from Task 6.1)_

|Source type
|`stackrox-alert`

|Skip TLS verify
|*Enabled* (lab environment uses self-signed certs)
|===
+
. Click *Test* to verify connectivity.
. Click *Save*.

== Task 6.3: Attach the Splunk notifier to a policy

. Navigate to *Platform Configuration* > *Policy Management*.
. Search for the policy: *Curl in Image*.
. Click the policy name to open its details.
. Click *Actions* > *Edit policy*.
. Navigate to the *Policy behavior* section (step 4).
. Under *Notification*, select *Lab - Splunk HEC* from the notifier dropdown.
. Click *Save*.

== Task 6.4: Trigger a violation and verify in Splunk

. On the bastion, create a deployment that will trigger the policy:
+
[source,bash]
----
oc create namespace splunk-test
oc run splunk-trigger --image=docker.io/library/ubuntu:20.04 --command -- sleep 3600 -n splunk-test
----
+
. Wait approximately 30 seconds for ACS to scan the image and evaluate the policy.
. Open the Splunk console.
. Click *Search & Reporting* in the left sidebar.
. Run the following search:
+
[source,text]
----
sourcetype="stackrox-alert"
----
+
. You should see one or more events from RHACS.
. Click an event and review the JSON payload:
** `alert.policy.name` — the policy that fired
** `alert.policy.severity` — severity level
** `alert.deployment.name` — the deployment that triggered it
** `alert.deployment.namespace` — where it runs

== Task 6.5: Discussion — Splunk dashboards for ACS

With events flowing into Splunk, you can build operational dashboards. Common patterns include:

* *Policy violation trends* — `sourcetype="stackrox-alert" | timechart count by alert.policy.severity`
* *Top violating namespaces* — `sourcetype="stackrox-alert" | top alert.deployment.namespace`
* *Critical policy breaches* — `sourcetype="stackrox-alert" alert.policy.severity="CRITICAL_SEVERITY" | table _time alert.policy.name alert.deployment.name`

These searches use standard Splunk SPL and can be saved as dashboard panels or scheduled alerts.

== Key takeaways

* The RHACS Splunk integration uses HEC (HTTP Event Collector) to push events in real time.
* Events include full policy, deployment, and violation context in structured JSON.
* The `sourcetype` field (`stackrox-alert`) makes it easy to isolate ACS events in Splunk searches.
* Splunk dashboards can provide SOC teams with real-time visibility into policy compliance across clusters.
