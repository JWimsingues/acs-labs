= Exercise 4: Risk profiling and CVE deferral
:navtitle: Exercise 4: Risk and CVE deferral

Estimated time: 15 minutes.

ACS computes a *Risk Priority* for each deployment from policy violations, vulnerabilities, configuration, and related signals.
SST and DSI teams use that ranking to decide what to fix first, and use *vulnerability exceptions* (deferral or false positive) when a CVE cannot be fixed immediately but needs an auditable record.

== Task 4.1: Analyze deployment risk factors

. In the RHACS Console, open *Risk* in the left navigation.
. Sort or scan the list for a high-priority application workload.
+
On this Showroom, `juice-shop` in namespace `juice-shop` is a reliable high-priority example. `log4shell` and workloads in `payments` are also good candidates.
. Click the deployment name.
. Review the risk breakdown panels:
** Policy violations (for example privileged settings or dangerous capabilities)
** Vulnerable images and CVE severity
** Configuration and exposure signals shown for that deployment
. Note one factor that would matter to platform operations and one that would matter to application owners.

== Task 4.2: Add a policy scope exclusion

Excluding non-production or sandbox workloads from a noisy policy recalibrates what appears as risk without disabling the policy globally.

. Navigate to *Platform Configuration* > *Policy Management*.
. Open *Fixable Severity at least Important* (enabled by default on this cluster).
. Click *Actions* > *Edit policy*.
. Open the *Policy scope* step.
. Add an exclusion for a namespace you are willing to carve out of this policy for the demo.
+
Example: exclude namespace `frontend` (or another non-critical app namespace in your environment).
. Save the policy.
. Return to *Risk* and refresh the view.
. Confirm that the excluded namespace's deployments are no longer driven by that policy's findings the same way (violations for that policy should stop accumulating there).

IMPORTANT: After the exercise, remove the exclusion (or set the policy back to its previous scope) so later labs and other students are not affected.

== Task 4.3: Defer a CVE with an auditable rationale

. Navigate to *Vulnerability Management* > *Results*.
. Switch to the *CVE* view if needed.
. Filter for a *Critical* and *Fixable* CVE on an application image (juice-shop or log4shell images are good sources).
. Open the CVE and choose *Defer* (wording may appear as *Request deferral* or *Defer CVE* depending on your role).
. Set:
** Duration: 30 days (or until a fixed version is available, if offered)
** Rationale: `Lab deferral: vendor fix under evaluation; compensating controls documented for DSI review.`
. Submit the request.
+
On this cluster, Admin can complete the workflow. If your user only has *Vulnerability Management Requester*, an Approver must approve before the exception becomes active.
. Confirm the CVE shows *Deferred* (or appears under vulnerability exceptions) and is no longer treated as an active blocking finding for that scope.

NOTE: This environment may already contain sample exceptions such as a deferred Log4Shell CVE. Create a *new* deferral for a different CVE so you can see the full request flow.

== Key takeaways

* Risk priority aggregates multiple signals so teams do not triage from a flat CVE list alone.
* Policy scope exclusions keep production gates strict while reducing noise from sandboxes.
* CVE deferral creates an auditable SST or DSI decision instead of silently ignoring a finding.
