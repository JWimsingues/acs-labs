Estimated time: 15 minutes.

RHACS continuously scans every image running in your clusters.
*Vulnerability Management* aggregates those results so you can triage by workload, image, or individual CVE.

Under the hood, *Scanner V4* splits the work into two stages:

* *Indexer*: generates a Software Bill of Materials (SBOM) by unpacking each image layer and cataloguing installed packages (RPM, APK, dpkg) and language-level dependencies (Java JARs, Go modules, Python wheels, Node packages).
* *Matcher*: correlates that SBOM against vulnerability feeds (NVD, Red Hat OVAL/RHSA, Alpine SecDB, Debian Security Tracker) to produce the final CVE list.

Base OS vulnerabilities and application-level vulnerabilities are evaluated through different feeds, so coverage and timing can differ.

== Task 1.1: Explore vulnerability results

. In the RHACS Console, expand *Vulnerability Management* and click *Results*.
. The default tab is *User Workloads*. Use the top-right dropdown to select *All vulnerable images* if you want inactive or watched images later.
. Narrow the view:
** *CVE severity:* *Critical*
** *CVE status:* fixable CVEs only
. Review columns such as *CVE*, *Images by severity*, *Top CVSS*, and *Top NVD CVSS*.
. Click a CVE to see affected deployments and images.

== Task 1.2: Drill into an image (OS vs language)

. On *Results*, switch the entity dropdown from *CVE* to *Image*.
. Open a high-count image from the demo apps. Good candidates on this cluster:
** `docker.io/bkimminich/juice-shop:latest` (namespace `juice-shop`)
** images used by `log4shell` or `apache-struts`
. In the image CVE table, note:
** *Fixable*
** *CVSS Score*
** *Component*
** *Source* (*OS* vs *Language*)
. Identify at least one OS-layer CVE and one language-level CVE.

TIP: Distroless or UBI Micro bases often show most findings under *Language*.

== Task 1.3: On-demand scan with watched images

. From *Vulnerability Management* > *Results*, click *Manage watched images*.
. Click *Add image* and enter:
+
[source,text]
----
docker.io/library/nginx:1.21
----
. Save and wait for the scan to finish.
. Return to *Results*, choose *Inactive images* (or the watched-image view), and open `nginx:1.21`.
. Compare severity distribution with a running demo image such as juice-shop.

NOTE: Watched images keep scanning until you remove them. Use that for pre-promotion checks.

== Key takeaways

* *Results* is the unified triage surface for deploy-time vulnerability data.
* Scanner V4 Indexer builds the SBOM. Matcher produces the CVE list.
* *Source* separates OS packages from language dependencies.
* Watched images let you evaluate candidates before they run in the cluster.
